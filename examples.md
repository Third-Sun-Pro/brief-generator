Project Overview

The purpose of this project is to create a new website for Animal Caretakers Impact Fund that communicates its unique mission to support those who care for animals and to clarify the organization's role within the veterinarian industry. The new site will drive engagement and interest from nonprofits, educators, potential partners, and the general public, helping visitors quickly understand what the Fund does—and what it does not do—while showcasing the impact it makes.

Animal Caretakers Impact Fund provides targeted grants to nonprofits that align with its mission: keeping animals with their humans, supporting veterinary and animal care professionals, and public education about the challenges veterinarians face while working in the industry. The Fund’s strengths include its empathetic, fair, and enduring approach to supporting the humans who care for animals, not just animals themselves.

Goals
- Clearly communicate Animal Caretakers Impact Fund’s value proposition, illustrating how it uniquely supports nonprofits and people in animal care roles.
- Clarify eligibility and dispel misconceptions by defining what the Fund does (and does not) fund.
- Build trust and inspire action among core audiences, including prospective grantees, educators, and community allies.
Core Users/Audience
- Nonprofit leaders and staff (especially executive directors, development coordinators) whose missions align with the Fund and who may apply for grants.
- Veterinary educators and administrators seeking new resources or partnerships.
- Potential board members and donors interested in animal welfare.
- The general public, looking for information on how animal caretakers are supported.
Calls to Action (CTAs) Examples
- Apply for a Grant
- Contact Us
- Partner With Us
Personality

The typical visitor to this site is a mission-driven professional in the animal welfare space—likely a nonprofit director, development coordinator, or educator—who is resourceful, time-pressed, and looking for clear information about grant opportunities. They're pragmatic problem-solvers who understand that supporting animal caretakers is essential to animal welfare. These visitors need to quickly determine if their organization aligns with the Fund's mission and easily apply for a grant if they do. 

Concerns / Misconceptions to Address
- Clarify that the Fund focuses on supporting those who help animals (animal caretakers), not direct care for animals or funding for vet clinics/individuals.
- Address confusion about funding eligibility and provide clear criteria for applicants.
- Explain why supporting animal caretakers is critical for animal welfare as a whole.

Design Notes

Creative Brief - 2026 Mosquito Abatement District
Third Sun proposes the following creative plan for Salt Lake City Mosquito Abatement District. Please review closely and provide feedback by leaving comments in this document by January 23, 2026. From there, we will solidify the assignments and timeline.

Target Date for Launch: Spring 2026

Current site: https://www.slcmadutah.gov/

Project Overview

The purpose of this project is to create a modern, science-backed, and user-friendly website for Salt Lake City Mosquito Abatement District that clearly communicates their world-class surveillance and control operations to protect public health.
 
Salt Lake City Mosquito Abatement District is a globally recognized leader in mosquito surveillance, research, and integrated mosquito management. The District protects Salt Lake City residents from mosquito-borne diseases and mosquito nuisance through science-driven methods that are safe, effective, and environmentally responsible. Their operations include comprehensive surveillance programs, targeted control interventions, cutting-edge research, public education, and professional training.

Goals
- Communicate the District's value proposition as a world-class, science-based operation that protects public health and enhances quality of life
- Make service requests intuitive and accessible, reducing friction for residents seeking mosquito control services
- Provide transparent access to surveillance data, treatment information, and research findings
- Dispel misconceptions about mosquito control methods and demonstrate environmental stewardship
- Showcase the District's research excellence and global leadership in the field
Core Users/Audience

Salt Lake City residents (primary): homeowners, renters, property managers seeking mosquito control services. All ages, genders, demographics, income levels
Community members: concerned about pesticide safety and environmental impact
Educational institutions: K-12 students, college students, educators requesting programming, tours, or classroom activities
Partners and collaborators: peer mosquito control districts, health departments, research institutions, government agencies
Job seekers (very small audience): approximately 40 seasonal job applicants annually


Calls to Action (CTAs) Examples
- Request Service
- View Treatment Map
- Check Mosquito Activity
- See Current Virus Data
- Learn About Our Methods
- Schedule an Educational Program
- Request No-Spray Zone
- Contact Us
Personality

The typical visitor to this site is a Salt Lake City resident dealing with mosquito issues—either frustrated by bites in their backyard or concerned about spray notices. They need fast answers: Can you help me? Is this safe? What area do you cover? They may be skeptical about pesticides or confused about what the District actually does. Some are educators seeking programs, researchers looking for data, or seasonal job applicants.

The website voice should be:

- Confident and authoritative: backed by science and global expertise, without arrogance
- Clear and accessible: "mosquito control products" not "adulticides"; concise language for the public 
- Transparent and accountable: showing data, methods, and real-time information openly
- Environmentally conscious: emphasizing stewardship, safety, and integrated pest management
- Responsive and service-oriented: here to serve you, funded by your taxes, acting quickly on your behalf
Concerns / Misconceptions to Address
- "You're poisoning the environment/wetlands/bees": Explain that the District uses ultra-low volume (ULV) treatments at EPA-approved rates, far lower than agricultural applications. Emphasize integrated mosquito management, data-driven interventions, and environmental stewardship
- "You only spray chemicals": Clarify that surveillance drives all operations. The District uses larvicides, biological control, habitat modification, and adult control only when surveillance data indicates necessity
- Service area confusion: Prominently display that the District serves Salt Lake City only, with clear direction to neighboring districts for those outside the coverage area
- "Why don't you just use bats/dragonflies/birds?": Address biological control myths and explain science-based methods
- "You only care about killing mosquitoes": Showcase passion for the environment, non-target organisms, and even non-biting mosquito species
- Product safety and autism/health concerns: Provide clear, accessible information about product safety, application rates, toxicology, and environmental fate
Additional Design Info
Design Notes
- Clean, modern, and professional aesthetic that reflects scientific rigor and global leadership
- Prioritize clarity over clutter—concise, clean, and clear
- Incorporate imagery of mosquito control operations, staff in action, laboratory work, and surveillance activities
- Make navigation intuitive for all tech literacy levels
